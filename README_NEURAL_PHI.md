# NEURAL PHI

### Temporal Organization of Language Networks

**Research project / hypothesis framework**

---

## Overview

**NEURAL PHI** investigates whether temporal organization in human language-related neural networks may contain reproducible mathematical relationships involving the golden ratio (φ).

The project focuses particularly on the language-associated cortical regions **Brodmann area 44 (BA44)** and **Brodmann area 45 (BA45)** and proposes experimentally testable temporal hypotheses.

The project is intentionally framed as a **research hypothesis**, not as an established scientific finding.

---

## Scientific Background

Michael Lacy's 2026 study, *Schumann-anchored golden ratio organization of human neural oscillations*, reports evidence for a φⁿ-related organization of human neural oscillations with a reference frequency of approximately **7.6 Hz**.

The present project takes a more anatomically and functionally specific approach.

Rather than assuming that a mathematical relationship exists, NEURAL PHI proposes to test whether comparable temporal relationships can be detected in neural activity associated with language processing, particularly in BA44 and BA45.

### Important distinction

The following elements are **not established by Lacy's work**:

- a specific BA44/BA45 localization of a φ-based temporal mechanism,
- the value **1.242705 s** as a validated neural constant,
- a causal relationship between φ and language processing.

These are proposed research questions of the present project.

---

## Core Research Question

> Do language-related neural signals in BA44 and BA45 exhibit reproducible temporal relationships that can be described by φ-based models and that provide explanatory value beyond appropriate control models?

---

## Main Hypotheses

### H1 — φ-related temporal organization

Temporal relationships within or between language-related neural signals may show statistically reproducible relationships involving:

**φ = 1.6180339887…**

### H2 — Candidate temporal value

The project proposes **1.242705 s** as a candidate temporal value to be tested experimentally.

This value is treated as a **predefined hypothesis**, not as a demonstrated biological constant.

### H3 — Language specificity

If a relevant temporal relationship exists, it should show stronger evidence during language-related processing than during suitable control conditions.

### H4 — Replication

Any reported effect should be reproducible across participants, recording sessions, and—where possible—independent datasets.

---

## Relationship to 7.6 Hz

A central reference in the scientific background is approximately:

**f₀ ≈ 7.6 Hz**

The corresponding period is:

**T₀ = 1 / 7.6 ≈ 0.131579 s**

Lacy's work investigates a φⁿ organization of neural oscillatory frequencies around this reference.

NEURAL PHI does **not** assume that 1.242705 s is simply a φⁿ transformation of 7.6 Hz. Instead, the project treats the value as a separate candidate temporal parameter whose relationship to neural dynamics must be tested empirically.

---

## Proposed Experimental Approach

A future experimental study could combine:

- EEG and/or intracranial recordings where ethically and clinically appropriate
- anatomically localized measurements of BA44 and BA45
- language and speech tasks
- matched non-language control conditions
- precise temporal event markers
- cross-frequency and temporal-relationship analysis
- statistical comparison against appropriate null and control models
- independent validation datasets

The precise experimental design should be determined before data collection and, ideally, preregistered.

---

## Falsification Criteria

The hypothesis should be considered unsupported if:

1. φ-based models do not outperform appropriate null/control models.
2. The proposed 1.242705 s effect is absent or unstable.
3. Effects disappear after correction for multiple comparisons.
4. Results cannot be replicated in independent data.
5. A simpler model explains the observations equally well or better.
6. The apparent effect depends on post-hoc parameter optimization.

The project therefore emphasizes **falsifiability and independent validation**.

## Research Principles

NEURAL PHI follows several principles:

- **Hypothesis before conclusion**
- **Predefined parameters where possible**
- **Falsifiability**
- **Appropriate control models**
- **Correction for multiple comparisons**
- **Independent validation**
- **Transparent distinction between evidence and speculation**

The project does not claim that a φ-based mechanism in BA44/BA45 has already been demonstrated.

---

## Project Goal

The long-term goal is to determine experimentally whether mathematical temporal organization can contribute measurable explanatory power to the dynamics of human language networks.

If such an effect exists, it should survive rigorous statistical testing, anatomical localization, appropriate controls, and independent replication.

If it does not, the hypothesis should be rejected or revised.
